Why it matters

A missing allergen on a label is not a typo. U.S. law requires labels to name the source of all 9 major allergens, and the FDA treats a product with an undeclared one as misbranded and subject to recall (FDA). For someone with a food allergy it can mean a trip to the emergency room.

How it happens: the label stays the same, the ingredient doesn't

Regulators name it as a root cause. Australia's food regulator counted 197 undeclared-allergen recalls in 2021–2025 and names two causes that match this exactly: a supplier changes an ingredient without the change being communicated, and label errors from incorrect translation or reformulation (FSANZ). Why today's tools miss this moment

The supplier change and the printed label live in different systems.

Artwork proofreading tools

Compare new artwork against the approved label text. If the approved text itself is out of date because a supplier changed an ingredient, a wrong label still matches and passes.

Supplier document platforms

Collect and extract supplier specs, usually by uploading them to a cloud platform. Specs and formulas are trade secrets, and the link to each printed label revision is a separate step.

LabelGate

Starts from the supplier change, finds every approved label that uses it, and blocks the wrong ones before print. The confidential document is read on the device, with no cloud fallback.
