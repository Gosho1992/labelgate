HOW IT WORKS

One supplier change. Every affected label checked. Nothing uploaded.

Gemini Nano reads the documents on the device. Fixed rules decide. A named QA reviewer confirms every reading and signs off every new artwork. Every decision is recorded in a log that detects later edits.

The workflow: two gates before print

Example from the demo: a seasoning supplier sends a new Spanish spec. A "natural flavour" now carries milk. Three products use that seasoning.

Supplier change arrivesA photographed or scanned spec sheet, in English, Spanish or Japanese.
Gate 1: read on deviceGemini Nano reads the photo through Firebase AI Logic ONLY_ON_DEVICE. The text streams in as it is read.
A person confirmsA named QA reviewer checks the reading against the photo and corrects it if needed.
Impact in millisecondsFixed rules re-check every product label that uses the ingredient: PRINT BLOCKED NEEDS REVIEW CHECKS CLEAR
Gate 2: new artworkThe corrected artwork is read on device, checked with neutral A/B questions, and QA signs off that exact revision.

Demo result: "Supplier added Milk → 2 labels PRINT BLOCKED · 1 clear", in 6–13 ms after the reading was confirmed.

Who decides what

AI is good at reading messy photos in other languages. It is not trusted to make a safety decision.

Gemini Nano reads

It turns the supplier photo and the label artwork into text, on the device. It never decides whether a label is safe.

Fixed rules decide

A deterministic check of 9 major U.S. allergens, matched in the original language too (leche, soja, 乳, 大豆, 小麦). "May contain" never counts as a declaration.

A person confirms

A named reviewer confirms every reading and signs off every artwork revision. A clear result is decision support, not regulatory approval.

Hybrid AI with a privacy boundary

Firebase AI Logic chooses where the model runs. LabelGate chooses the mode by how sensitive the data is.

Private: supplier specs, label artwork, formulas

InferenceMode.ONLY_ON_DEVICE with image input. A privacy guard checks inferenceSource on every streamed chunk and on the final answer, and the app lists every outside request made during the read. Without Gemini Nano the read stops and the reviewer types the reading. No cloud fallback.

Public: recall notices

InferenceMode.PREFER_ON_DEVICE with a JSON response schema. Gemini Nano when available; otherwise gemini-3.5-flash-lite in the cloud, protected by Firebase App Check (reCAPTCHA Enterprise). The notice is then matched to your own formulas locally.

Which model ran: on the web an app cannot choose the on-device model; Chrome picks it for the hardware. On our test laptop Chrome installed nano_v3_gpu_low_tier_model, so our Chrome results ran on Gemini Nano v3. We also switched on Chrome's "Gemma 4 for Built-in AI" flag on that laptop to test the newer model; it had not finished downloading when we submitted, so LabelGate has not yet run on it. Screenshots.

Also: the model is warmed up when the page opens (cold start ≈ 38 s → warm ≈ 2 s), and every AI call is logged with where it ran, how long it took and the bytes sent to the cloud.

Every feature

Reading

  • On-device reading of photographed specs and label artwork
  • English, Spanish and Japanese
  • Live streaming text
  • Empty reading retried once, then blocked
  • Placeholders ("Otros: no aplica") and mixed-alphabet words flagged

Rules

  • 9 major U.S. allergens, deterministic
  • Original-language allergen words
  • Look-alikes excluded (coconut milk, 乳化剤)
  • Facility and shared-line wording is not an ingredient
  • "Contains" lines found by a rule, not the model

Impact

  • Supplier Change Impact Card with a one-line verdict
  • Exact wording that changed and where it is on the spec
  • Every affected label re-checked in milliseconds
  • Next action for each label

Review & sign-off

  • Neutral A/B questions, random order, nothing pre-selected
  • "I can't tell" sends the label to review
  • Sign-off tied to the artwork's SHA-256 fingerprint
  • Unchanged labels re-approved for the new supplier revision

Audit

  • Hash-chained log of every decision
  • "Test tamper detection" button
  • Export as JSON or CSV
  • Verified label library with reviewer, date and supplier revision

Hybrid & platform

  • Public recall notices: on device or cloud fallback
  • Privacy evidence after every private read
  • Works offline once the page is open
  • Guided 2-minute demo; "type it myself" mode without Nano
  • Android: the same on-device reading on a real Galaxy S26

How we built it

Nov 25 · Does Nano run at all?
Gemini Nano tested on a CPU-only AWS Windows VM, then benchmarked on held-out cases against a rules-only baseline. Finding: Nano reads unfamiliar wording well but cannot be trusted to classify, so it became the reader, not the judge.
Nov 26 · The real Firebase path
Firebase AI Logic, App Check and Hosting connected; the same benchmark through Firebase AI Logic (60/60). Photo reading proven on the laptop: 22/22 on device. Acceptance set A failed, root causes fixed; fresh set B passed 7/7.
Nov 27 · People, offline, Android
Two human blind tests (the first failed and forced a redesign), offline test, live reading glitch turned into a rule, Android reading on a real Galaxy S26 via Samsung Remote Test Lab, independent comprehension reviews.

Platforms, users and honest limits

Platforms

  • Desktop Chrome: the full product, live now.
  • Android: the private on-device reading already runs on a real Galaxy S26 through Firebase AI Logic. Next: the full workflow in the Android app.

Users

No public users yet. People tested it in blind reviewer tests and comprehension reviews. Next: a pilot with a real food manufacturer's QA team.

Limits we state openly

  • On-device reads take 30–90 s on a laptop without a GPU (one read per supplier change).
  • Small, synthetic test sets; few human testers.
  • Nano can misspell; that is why a person confirms every reading.
  • Simplified U.S. allergen rules; single user, browser storage.

Try it yourself

Desktop Chrome with Gemini Nano gives the full on-device experience. The guided demo takes about 2 minutes plus reading time.
